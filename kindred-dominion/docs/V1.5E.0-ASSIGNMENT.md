# Kindred Dominion — V1.5E.0
## Player Trading Hall Architecture and Implementation Readiness

Repository: `Revty79/kindereddominion`

This assignment prepares the approved future player-to-player trading system.

Player trading is intended to become a permanent feature of Kindred Dominion, supporting its identity as a collectible tactical card game and contributing to player retention.

## 1. Execution boundary

Complete this assignment only after reporting the status of all preceding assigned milestones.

If V1.5C or V1.5D remains unimplemented or unaccepted, limit this assignment to non-mutating architectural analysis and planning.

Do not activate player trading, modify production inventories, deploy changes or process commercial transactions.

## 2. Approved design direction

The future Trading Hall should support:

- Direct player-to-player trade offers.
- Public trade listings.
- Player wish lists.
- Multiple cards within individual offers.
- Counteroffers.
- Trade acceptance and rejection.
- Trade cancellation and expiration.
- Trade history.
- Search by Kindred, collection, rarity and card identity.
- Trade-related notifications.
- Clearly presented ownership changes.
- Server-authoritative inventory transfers.

Player trading must remain card-for-card initially.

Real-money player marketplaces, cash withdrawals, auction houses and player currency transfers are not authorized.

## 3. Existing architecture review

Inspect the accepted economy and ownership infrastructure.

Determine whether it adequately supports:

- Permanent gameplay card identities.
- Multiple owned copies.
- Acquisition provenance.
- Lifetime first-copy acquisition.
- Account-bound cards.
- Configurable tradeability.
- Inventory reservations.
- Concurrent ownership changes.
- Immutable transaction history.
- Future payment refunds and chargebacks.

Identify missing prerequisites and propose compatible migrations.

Do not replace functioning ownership infrastructure unnecessarily.

## 4. Economic integrity

Evaluate and propose safeguards against:

- Mass account creation to obtain free starter cards.
- Farming free first-copy rewards for transfer to another account.
- Repeated acquisition of previously traded-away cards.
- Automated trading.
- Fraudulent purchases followed by rapid transfers.
- Multi-account exploitation.
- Concurrent acceptance of conflicting offers.
- Unauthorized inventory mutations.
- Trades involving cards that have become unavailable.
- Manipulated client-side trade requests.

Evaluate account-bound starter grants and configurable tradeability restrictions for ordinary progression cards.

Preserve meaningful opportunities for legitimate free players to obtain and trade additional copies.

Propose reasonable account eligibility requirements without unnecessarily restricting legitimate new players.

## 5. Transaction architecture

Design an atomic, server-authoritative trade settlement process.

Both sides of an accepted trade must settle together or neither side may settle.

Address:

- Offer versioning.
- Ownership verification.
- Inventory reservation.
- Reservation expiration.
- Transaction locking.
- Idempotency.
- Simultaneous acceptance attempts.
- Interrupted requests.
- Application restarts.
- Trade cancellation.
- Administrative investigation.
- Auditable corrective actions.

Specify what happens when a player trades away a card currently referenced by one or more saved decks.

Saved decks should not silently change their contents.

Instead, propose clear invalid-deck indicators and appropriate validation before entering a match.

Active matches must remain independent of subsequent collection changes.

## 6. Player interface design

Prepare a proposed Trading Hall interface consistent with the existing application.

Include:

- Trading dashboard.
- Public listings.
- Personal offers.
- Collection-based card selection.
- Wish lists.
- Offer creation.
- Counteroffers.
- Side-by-side trade comparison.
- Final confirmation.
- Trade history.
- Clear ownership previews.
- Trade eligibility explanations.
- Responsive browser behavior.
- Appropriate accessibility.

Do not implement an external player messaging system or unrestricted chat without separate authorization.

## 7. Administrative controls

Design configurable administration tools supporting:

- Trading activation and suspension.
- Trade eligibility.
- Account restrictions.
- Tradeability policies.
- Holding periods.
- Offer expiration.
- Trade limits.
- Suspicious activity review.
- Transaction investigation.
- Audit records.
- Emergency trade suspension.

Administrative intervention must not silently rewrite ownership histories.

## 8. Commercial and physical product compatibility

Consider future compatibility with:

- Stripe-funded purchases.
- Mobile platform purchases.
- Refunds and chargebacks.
- Premium collections.
- Alternate artwork ownership.
- Future physical collectible card products.
- Individually serialized physical cards.
- Potential NFC-enabled card authentication and digital redemption.

The physical card concept is exploratory only.

Do not implement NFC readers, hardware integrations, physical card redemption or manufacturing systems during this assignment.

Identify architectural decisions that would preserve these future opportunities without overengineering the current application.
