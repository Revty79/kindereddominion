- Fraud-restricted or disputed assets are unavailable for trading.

Do not create a system in which only paying players can participate in meaningful trading.

## 6. Inventory reservation system

Implement reusable inventory reservations.

Reservations must support:

- Account ownership.
- Permanent card identity.
- Acquisition lot or equivalent provenance reference.
- Quantity.
- Reservation purpose.
- Associated offer.
- Creation timestamp.
- Expiration timestamp.
- Release status.

A reserved card copy cannot be simultaneously committed to multiple incompatible offers.

Reservations must not destroy ownership.

Prevent negative available inventory.

Expired, cancelled and declined offers must release applicable reservations.

Reservations must be recoverable after application restarts.

Use server-side transactional locking to prevent concurrent inventory inconsistencies.

Document whether public listings reserve inventory immediately or only when converted into a concrete offer.

Prefer non-binding public listings, with authoritative inventory reservation when a player makes a specific trade offer.

## 8. Trade offer lifecycle

Implement explicit offer states supporting:

- Draft.
- Pending.
- Countered.
- Accepted.
- Declined.
- Cancelled.
- Expired.
- Settled.
- Restricted or invalidated.

Define permitted state transitions.

A counteroffer must produce a new immutable offer version.

Previous confirmations must not apply to modified trade contents.

The initiating player's final submission constitutes consent to the exact submitted offer.

The recipient must explicitly confirm that exact offer before settlement.

Any counteroffer requires renewed consent from the opposite participant.

Prevent settlement of stale, cancelled, expired or superseded offers.

Use server-authoritative timestamps and state validation.

## 9. Atomic trade settlement

Implement an atomic settlement service.

At settlement time:

1. Authenticate the accepting player.
2. Verify that trading is enabled.
3. Verify account eligibility.
4. Verify both participants.
5. Verify the exact offer version.
6. Verify offer expiration and state.
7. Lock relevant inventory and offer records in a deterministic order.
8. Verify ownership quantities.
9. Verify acquisition provenance.
10. Verify tradeability restrictions.
11. Verify relevant inventory reservations.
12. Verify that neither side violates configured limits.
13. Transfer both sides within the same database transaction.
14. Record immutable acquisition and transfer history.
15. Release applicable reservations.
16. Mark the trade settled.
17. Generate post-commit notifications.

Both sides must settle together or neither side may settle.

Support idempotent settlement requests.

Repeated acceptance requests must not duplicate cards.

Failed settlements must not remove cards from either participant.

Never trust client-submitted ownership balances, tradeability states or transfer quantities without server-side verification.

Notifications must not be able to invalidate an otherwise successful trade.
