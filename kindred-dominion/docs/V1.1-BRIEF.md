# KINDRED DOMINION
## Development Stage V1.1 — Playable Foundation

## 1. Project Objective

Create a new standalone, browser-based tactical collectible card game named **Kindred Dominion**, inspired by territorial card games such as Queen's Blood, but with independently developed rules, mechanics, terminology and presentation.

Project identifiers:

- Game name: Kindred Dominion
- Project folder: `kindred-dominion`
- Initial application version: `0.1.0`
- Development stage: V1.1 — Playable Foundation
- Preferred local development port: 5173
- Initial platform: Web browser
- Future platforms: Android and iOS

This is a completely independent project. Do not modify Serrian Tide or any other existing project.

The initial objective is a functional application that the owner can run locally on a Windows computer and use to play and test actual matches.

Mobile packaging, public hosting and commercial infrastructure will be developed later.

## 2. Technology and Architecture

Use:

- React.
- TypeScript.
- Vite.
- Local browser storage where persistence is necessary.
- A framework-independent, deterministic game rules engine.
- Data-driven card definitions.
- Configurable battlefield and tactical ruleset definitions.
- Automated unit and integration tests.

Keep gameplay logic independent from the user interface so it can eventually support authoritative multiplayer servers and mobile applications.

Do not require paid services, external AI APIs, databases, authentication or internet connectivity for local gameplay.

Design the application with future mobile packaging through Capacitor in mind.

## 3. Battlefield Initialization

The standard battlefield is three rows by five columns, containing 15 playable positions.

Player One begins with ownership of the entire leftmost column.

Player Two begins with ownership of the entire rightmost column.

Each starting position contains one placement token and zero fortification.

All remaining positions begin neutral, with zero placement tokens and zero fortification.

No creatures begin on the battlefield.

Starting territory receives no special protection.

Board dimensions, starting positions, initial tokens and fortification limits must be configurable rather than hard-coded.

Future battlefield formats may include 3×6, 4×5, 4×6, 4×8 and others.

## 4. Deck Construction

A playing deck contains exactly 15 cards.

Standard Play defaults to a maximum of two copies of each individual card definition, with configurable card-specific overrides.

Unrestricted Free Play permits up to 15 copies of an individual card.

Universal gameplay safeguards remain active in Free Play.

Do not enforce minimum rank distributions or mandatory ability types.

Support single-collection and mixed-collection deck validation.

Track individual card instances independently, including duplicate cards.

For development testing, provide unrestricted access to all implemented test cards and sufficient copies.

Actual card ownership and acquisition systems will be implemented later.

## 5. Opening Hand and Mulligan

Each player draws five cards.

Each player receives one optional mulligan.

The player may select zero to five cards to exchange.

Selected cards are temporarily removed from the hand and set aside.

Replacement cards are drawn before the rejected cards are shuffled back into the remaining draw pile.

The same individual rejected card cannot be immediately redrawn during that mulligan.

Neither player sees the other player's hand or mulligan selections.

There is no mulligan penalty.

## 6. Turn Management

Randomly determine the starting player for the first match.

Alternate starting players during rematches.

Neither player draws on their opening turn.

On subsequent turns, draw one card if the draw pile is not empty.

The standard turn sequence is:

1. Resolve beginning-of-turn effects.
2. Draw a card when applicable.
3. Play one legal card or pass.
4. Resolve territorial influence.
5. Resolve card abilities and triggered effects.
6. Resolve end-of-turn effects.
7. Update scores and check match completion.
8. Transfer control to the opponent.

A player receives one voluntary placement action per turn.

Ability-generated placements do not consume this allowance.

Replacement and evolution consume the standard placement action.

## 7. Territory System

Each battlefield position independently tracks:

- Ownership.
- Placement tokens.
- Fortification.
- Occupying creature, if any.

Placement tokens range from zero to three by default.

Fortification ranges from zero to three by default.

These maximum values must be configurable.

Support three territorial influence mechanics:

### Expansion

Claims neutral territory and increases friendly placement tokens.

Ordinary hostile expansion removes enemy fortification before capturing an unoccupied position.

Each influence point resolves sequentially.

An influence point consumed removing the final fortification does not also capture the position. A subsequent influence point is required.

Captured positions lose previous ownership, placement tokens and fortification. The capturing influence establishes the new placement tokens, subject to the configured maximum.

### Fortification

Increases the defensive fortification of friendly territory.

Fortification is independent of placement tokens.

### Overthrow

Immediately captures eligible unoccupied enemy territory regardless of its fortification.

Previous fortification and placement tokens are removed. New placement tokens are established according to the card's defined effect.

Ordinary territorial influence cannot capture or weaken occupied enemy positions.

Friendly occupied territory may receive additional placement tokens and fortification.

Individual cards may combine territorial mechanics across different directional patterns.

Influence strength must be configurable for each affected position.

Ignore out-of-bounds targets.

Directional patterns must correctly mirror for opposing players.

## 8. Standard Placement

Standard creatures require an empty friendly position containing at least their required placement rank.

Support Rank I, Rank II and Rank III.

Placement tokens and fortification are not consumed.

Lower-ranked creatures may occupy higher-ranked positions.

Destroying a creature leaves the underlying territory unchanged unless an explicit ability modifies it.

## 9. Replacement

Replacement cards normally replace any friendly creature.

Individual cards may define additional prerequisites.

Replacement constitutes destruction by sacrifice.

Record the sacrificed creature's destruction and death triggers.

Place the replacement creature before resolving the sacrificed creature's death abilities.

Then resolve the replacement creature's influence and abilities.

The incoming creature may benefit from eligible sacrifice-triggered effects.

Underlying territory, tokens and fortification remain intact.

Previous creature statistics, modifiers and abilities are not inherited unless explicitly specified.

Replacement creatures may themselves be replaced.

## 10. Evolution

Evolution is a distinct placement type.

Evolution prerequisites may specify:

- An exact predecessor.
- Multiple acceptable predecessors.
- Creature classifications.
- Other explicitly defined eligibility conditions.

Support branching and multistage evolution chains.

Evolution consumes the normal placement action and occupies the predecessor's position.

Evolution does not count as destruction or sacrifice.

Previous forms remain in an evolution stack.

Existing power modifiers, applicable statuses and match-long restrictions are retained.

The evolved creature applies its own influence and eligible abilities.

Evolution must not reset resurrection or activation limits.

When an evolved creature is destroyed, its entire evolution stack enters the graveyard.

Only the active creature triggers death abilities.

Evolution prerequisites must also be respected during resurrection unless explicitly overridden.

## 11. Card Lifecycle

Maintain distinct logical card locations:

- Draw pile.
- Hand.
- Battlefield.
- Graveyard.
- Evolution stack.
- Exile.

An individual card instance cannot exist in multiple locations simultaneously.

Distinguish destruction, sacrifice, evolution, return-to-hand and banishment events.

Creatures with current power of zero or below are destroyed.

Direct destruction effects may destroy creatures regardless of power unless an applicable protection prevents it.

Returning cards to hand and banishing cards do not trigger death abilities by default.

## 12. Ability Engine

Build reusable, data-driven effects and triggers.

Do not hard-code individual creature names into gameplay logic.

Initial effect support should include:

- Placement token modification.
- Fortification modification.
- Territory capture.
- Overthrow.
- Power enhancement.
- Power weakening.
- Direct destruction.
- Replacement.
- Evolution.
- Summoning.
- Limited resurrection.
- Card drawing and generation.
- Basic conditional abilities.
- Basic protection effects.

Support triggers including:

- On placement.
- On evolution.
- On replacement.
- On destruction.
- On sacrifice.
- Beginning of turn.
- End of turn.
- Other creature placement or destruction.

Keep the effect architecture extensible.

Individual mechanics that are not implemented must be explicitly identified rather than represented by nonfunctional placeholders.

## 13. Effect Resolution and Safeguards

Effects targeting multiple creatures resolve simultaneously.

All creatures reduced to zero or below by the same effect are removed before resolving their death abilities.

Prioritize the active player's simultaneous triggered abilities.

Resolve abilities within each player's group in a consistent, documented battlefield-coordinate order.

Resolve chains completely before accepting another voluntary player action.

Implement safeguards including:

- Per-instance activation tracking.
- Once-per-turn restrictions.
- Once-per-match restrictions.
- Once-per-event restrictions.
- Resurrection limits.
- Restrictions on resurrection-triggered abilities.
- Card-generation limits.
- Infinite-loop detection and controlled resolution limits.

Use persistent card-instance and creature-lineage tracking where appropriate so moving, evolving or resurrecting a creature does not reset restrictions improperly.

If an unexpected resolution loop occurs, report it clearly in the development log and prevent it from silently producing an invalid match result.

## 14. Match Completion

Players may pass voluntarily or be forced to pass when they have no legal plays.

Passing does not withdraw a player from the match.

Players may resume playing on later turns.

Two consecutive passes end the match after all pending effects have resolved.

Playing a card resets the consecutive-pass counter.

A full battlefield does not automatically end a match.

An empty draw pile does not cause defeat.

Replacement and evolution remain legal on full battlefields when their requirements are satisfied.

Players may surrender.

Alternative tactical rulesets may define additional completion conditions.

## 15. Initial Tactical Rulesets

Implement two selectable rulesets.

### Classic

Calculate each player's total surviving creature power in each horizontal row.

The player with greater power in that row receives their full row power as points.

The losing player receives zero points for that row.

Tied rows award zero points to both players.

Sum the winning row points.

### Domination

Use Classic scoring, then award one additional point for every surviving creature controlled by each player.

Display individual creature counts underneath each vertical column.

Sum these creature counts and add them to the Classic score.

Do not count previous evolutionary stages as separate surviving creatures.

The higher final score wins.

Equal final scores produce a draw.

Make scoring extensible for additional tactical rulesets.

## 16. Card Collections — The Founding Kindreds

The planned initial commercial card library consists of five collections, collectively referred to as the Founding Kindreds:

| Kindred | Primary specialization |
|---|---|
| Nature | Expansion, cooperation and population |
| Mythical | Power, destruction and resilience |
| Arcane | Manipulation, transformation and disruption |
| Undead | Sacrifice, resurrection and death effects |
| Constructs | Fortification, protection and defense |

Each collection will eventually contain 30 unique cards, for 150 total.

Each collection must ultimately be independently competitive while supporting mixed-collection deck construction.

For V1.1, create a provisional 30-card development library covering all five collection identities and all implemented core mechanics.

These are test cards, not approved commercial card definitions.

Provide two or more legal, functional 15-card test decks.

Include dedicated test definitions where necessary to exercise replacement, evolution, fortification, overthrow, simultaneous destruction and resurrection.

Do not expand the commercial card library or commission final artwork during this stage.
