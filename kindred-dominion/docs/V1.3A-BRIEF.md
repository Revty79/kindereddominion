### 4. Continuous Power Effects — Auras

Implement continuous enhancement and weakening effects.

A continuous effect remains active while its source creature is alive, present on the battlefield and satisfies its activation conditions.

Support effects targeting:

- The source creature.
- Friendly creatures.
- Enemy creatures.
- Both players' creatures.
- Specified relative positions.
- Adjacent creatures.
- Creatures in the same horizontal row.
- Creatures in the same vertical column.
- The entire battlefield.
- Specific classifications.
- Specific Kindreds.
- Specific card definitions.

Continuous effects must automatically apply to newly placed eligible creatures.

Continuous effects must automatically disappear when:

- Their source is destroyed.
- Their source is sacrificed.
- Their source is replaced.
- Their source evolves into a creature without that effect.
- Their source leaves the battlefield.
- Their activation conditions are no longer satisfied.
- A target is no longer eligible.

Continuous modifiers must be tracked separately from permanent power modifications.

Do not repeatedly apply an aura as permanent power every time the battlefield changes.

Example:

A Honeybee grants adjacent friendly creatures +1 power while it remains in play.

If the Honeybee is destroyed, its contribution disappears immediately.

Example:

A Basilisk continuously reduces enemy creatures within its targeting pattern by 2 power.

Newly placed enemies within that pattern receive the effect.

### 5. Dynamic Power Scaling

Support creature power that changes according to current battlefield conditions.

Allow a creature to gain or lose configurable power based on:

- Number of friendly creatures.
- Number of enemy creatures.
- Number of creatures of a specified classification.
- Number of creatures belonging to a specified Kindred.
- Number of enhanced creatures.
- Number of weakened creatures.
- Number of creatures in a particular row or column.
- Number of occupied or unoccupied positions.
- Number of controlled territories.
- Number of reinforced territories.
- Total placement tokens or fortification within eligible positions.
- Current power of eligible creatures, subject to safe dependency restrictions.
- Historical destruction and sacrifice events.
- Evolution stage or stack depth.

Support configurable minimums, maximums and scaling caps.

Dynamic bonuses must decrease or disappear when their qualifying conditions change.

Example:

An Alpha Wolf gains +1 power for every other friendly Wolf on the battlefield.

Its power decreases when those Wolves leave play.

Example:

A creature gains +2 power for every weakened enemy creature, up to a maximum bonus of +6.

The engine must distinguish dynamic scaling from permanent event-based growth.

### 6. Power Calculation Architecture

Establish a centralized, deterministic power calculation system.

Distinguish between:

1. Printed base power.
2. Permanent power modifiers.
3. Continuous aura modifiers.
4. Dynamic scaling modifiers.
5. Final effective power.

All gameplay systems must use the same authoritative effective-power calculation.

This includes:

- Card placement prerequisites.
- Destruction checks.
- Protection conditions.
- Ability targeting.
- Scoring.
- Computer opponents.
- Previews.
- Simulations.
- Replays.

For initial implementation, enhanced and weakened classifications used by dynamic scaling should be calculated from power before dynamic scaling contributions are applied.

This prevents two scaling abilities from recursively determining each other's power.

Document this distinction clearly.

Reject unsupported circular dependencies between continuous effects and dynamic calculations. Do not use an uncontrolled iterative calculation that can oscillate indefinitely.

Recalculate affected power values after relevant battlefield changes.

Resolve creatures reaching zero or negative effective power according to the existing simultaneous destruction rules.

Removing an aura may cause additional creatures to be destroyed. Such consequences must resolve completely before another voluntary action is accepted.

### 7. Expanded Reactive Triggers

Implement reusable reactive triggers for:

- When the source is enhanced.
- When the source is weakened.
- When another creature is enhanced.
- When another creature is weakened.
- When the source is first enhanced during a match.
- When the source is first weakened during a match.
- When a creature crosses a specified power threshold.
- When territory is claimed.
- When enemy territory is captured.
- When friendly territory is lost.
- When territory is fortified.
- When fortification is reduced.
- When a creature is resurrected.
- When a creature is banished.
- When a creature is returned to hand.
- When a card is drawn or generated.

Retain all existing triggers.

Triggers must support relationship filters, classification filters and other applicable conditions.

Example:

Whenever another friendly creature is weakened, this creature gains +1 permanent power, once per turn.

Example:

The first time this creature is enhanced during a match, it grants +2 power to adjacent allies.

Example:

When an enemy captures friendly territory, this creature fortifies specified friendly positions.

Reactive triggers must respond to real changes, not merely attempted effects.

An enhancement prevented by immunity, or fortification attempted against an already capped position, must not produce a successful-change trigger.

### 8. Power-Change Event Semantics

Record structured power-change events containing:

- Affected creature.
- Previous effective power.
- New effective power.
- Amount changed.
- Source of the change.
- Cause of the change.
- Whether the change was permanent or continuous.
- Whether the change resulted from dynamic scaling.
- Event identifier.

Allow reactive abilities to filter the cause of a power change.

By default, enhancement and weakening triggers should respond to explicit power-modifying effects. Recalculating unchanged continuous or dynamic modifiers must not generate additional events.

Allow explicit opt-in to reactions from actual aura or dynamic-power changes where safely supported.

Prevent an ability from repeatedly triggering itself through its own event chain.

### 9. Threshold and Conditional Abilities

Support abilities that activate when a specified condition becomes true.

Conditions should include:

- Power reaches or exceeds a threshold.
- Power falls below a threshold.
- Friendly creature count reaches a threshold.
- Enemy creature count reaches a threshold.
- Specified territory ownership.
- Specified placement-token count.
- Specified fortification count.
- Position within the battlefield.
- Presence of qualifying creatures.
- Presence of qualifying evolution stages.

Threshold abilities should normally activate on a false-to-true transition rather than repeatedly activating while the condition remains true.

Support once-per-match restrictions and explicitly configured repeatable behavior.

Allow multiple supported conditions to be combined using AND, OR and NOT.

Use a validated structured condition format rather than arbitrary executable scripts.

### 10. Variable-Strength Effects

Expand effects beyond fixed numerical amounts.

Allow effect magnitude to depend on:

- Source base power.
- Source effective power.
- Target base power.
- Target effective power.
- Sacrificed creature power.
- Destroyed creature power.
- Number of qualifying creatures.
- Number of qualifying territories.
- Historical event counts.
- Evolution stack depth.
- Other explicitly supported numerical values.

Support:

- Fixed amounts.
- Addition.
- Subtraction.
- Multiplication by a configured constant.
- Division with documented rounding.
- Minimum and maximum limits.

Resolve numerical expressions deterministically.

Capture relevant event values before removing or modifying their source creature.

Example:

A Venus Flytrap replaces a friendly creature and gains permanent power equal to half the sacrificed creature's effective power, rounded down.

Example:

A scavenger gains +1 power for each enemy creature destroyed during the match, up to +4.

No arbitrary formulas or executable scripts are permitted.

### 11. Replacement and Evolution Context

Extend replacement, sacrifice, destruction and evolution events to retain immutable information about their participants.

Include:

- Previous creature definition.
- Previous creature classification.
- Previous creature base power.
- Previous creature effective power.
- Permanent modifiers.
- Relevant protections.
- Evolution stack depth.
- Previous battlefield position.
- Cause of removal.
- Incoming creature identity where applicable.

Allow abilities to reference these values safely after the original creature has left play.

Preserve the approved replacement sequence.

Replacement constitutes sacrifice and triggers eligible death abilities.

Evolution does not constitute destruction or sacrifice.

Existing match-long and lineage restrictions must remain intact.

### 12. Expanded Scoring Abilities

Implement data-driven abilities that award score independently of creature power.

Support:

- Fixed bonus points while a creature survives.
- Additional points when its horizontal row is won.
- Additional points when its vertical column satisfies a condition.
- Bonus points for surviving in specified battlefield positions.
- Bonus points based on friendly creature population.
- Bonus points based on controlled territory.
- Bonus points based on fortified territory.
- Bonus points based on historical destruction or sacrifice events.

Support configurable caps.

Distinguish:

- Ordinary row power.
- Tactical ruleset points.
- Card-specific bonus points.
- Final score.

Determine ordinary row winners using effective creature power before applying card-specific scoring bonuses.

Scoring bonuses must not recursively change the conditions that awarded those bonuses.

Scoring must remain a pure calculation that does not generate gameplay actions or mutate battlefield state.

Support both Classic and Domination.

Do not implement additional full tactical rulesets during this stage.

### 14. Conditional Protection

Retain existing direct-destruction and weakening immunity.

Allow protection to be conditional or continuously granted by another creature.

Example:

A defensive creature protects adjacent allies from direct destruction while it remains alive.

Example:

A creature gains weakening immunity while occupying territory with at least two fortification.

Continuously granted protection must disappear when its source or qualifying condition is removed.

Protection must not prevent sacrifice, evolution or other mechanics unless explicitly defined and supported.

Maintain separate records for permanent and continuous protection.

- Reactive event processing.
- Threshold condition transitions.
- Additional ability chains.
- Final score calculation.
