# Kindred Dominion — V1.6D
## Authoritative Tactical Placement, Battlefield Influence and Ability Previews

Repository: `Revty79/kindereddominion`

Implement a comprehensive tactical preview system that allows players to understand the consequences of a proposed card placement before committing the action.

This milestone extends V1.6C interactive card selection and placement.

## 1. Prerequisites and boundaries

Inspect the actual implementations of:

- Existing authoritative gameplay engine.
- Placement validation.
- Card influence patterns.
- Card abilities.
- Evolution mechanics.
- Power and scoring rules.
- Persistent match state.
- V1.6C pointer and touchscreen interactions.

Preserve established gameplay mechanics.

Do not introduce new abilities or alter existing card balance merely to support presentation.

Do not create an independent client-side approximation of the gameplay engine.

Reuse authoritative rule evaluation or shared deterministic engine logic.

## 2. Non-mutating action preview

Implement a reusable gameplay action preview capability.

Given the current match state, selected card and proposed destination, determine the visible consequences of that action without committing it.

The preview must not:

- Modify persistent match state.
- Consume a card.
- Modify placement tokens.
- Advance the turn.
- Advance authoritative randomness.
- Grant experience or currency.
- Trigger achievements or challenges.
- Generate actual match events.
- Reveal hidden opponent information.

The preview must be safe to request repeatedly.

Use a match-state revision or equivalent mechanism to prevent stale previews from being presented as current.

## 3. Placement legality

When a player selects a card, clearly identify all destinations where that card can legally be placed.

Distinguish:

- Legal placements.
- Insufficient placement requirements.
- Opponent-controlled destinations.
- Occupied destinations.
- Valid evolution targets.
- Other existing special placement exceptions.

Provide concise explanations for invalid destinations.

Use existing rules rather than inventing simplified presentation rules.

Selecting an invalid destination must not submit a gameplay action.

## 4. Hover and target previews

While a selected card follows the pointer, update the preview based on the targeted battlefield tile.

For touchscreen users, provide an equivalent preview through touch targeting or a selected destination.

Show:

- The proposed placement location.
- The selected card's influence pattern.
- Every affected battlefield tile.
- Relevant existing cards affected by the action.
- Directional relationships where useful.
- Placement token changes.
- Ownership changes.
- Ability effects.
- Evolution opportunities.
- Predicted visible scoring changes where deterministically knowable.

The floating selected card must not obscure the target or important affected tiles.

Clear the preview when the player cancels selection, leaves the battlefield or commits an action.

## 5. Effect classifications

Provide distinct reusable presentation categories for applicable existing mechanics.

Support visual communication of:

- Placement token increases.
- Placement token decreases, if supported.
- Territory ownership changes.
- Friendly card power increases.
- Enemy card power reductions.
- Card destruction.
- Card protection.
- Card replacement.
- Evolution.
- Conditional abilities.
- Other currently implemented gameplay effects.

Inspect the actual card definitions and engine capabilities before deciding which categories are active.

Do not introduce unsupported gameplay mechanics.

Multiple effects on the same tile must remain understandable.

Provide icons, numerical changes and accessible text rather than relying exclusively on color.

## 6. Numerical previews

Display readable numerical changes directly on affected tiles or cards.

Examples include:

- +1 placement token.
- +2 power.
- -2 power.
- Current value changing to a predicted new value.

Clearly distinguish placement token values from card power and scoring values.

Do not make temporary preview values appear to be already committed.

Preserve the larger battlefield placement numbers introduced in V1.6C.

Do not alter the printed statistics on the full-bleed card artwork renderer.

## 7. Ability explanations

Provide an unobtrusive preview summary explaining why affected tiles and cards are highlighted.

Where deterministically known, explain:

- Which ability will activate.
- What condition has been satisfied.
- Which card or tile will be affected.
- What numerical change is expected.
- Whether a card will be destroyed or evolved.
- Which effects depend on subsequent conditions.

Use readable language suitable for new players.

Reuse the actual ability definitions and authoritative resolution logic.

Avoid obscuring the battlefield with large mandatory popups.

Provide expanded inspection when requested.

## 8. Conditional, hidden and randomized effects

Never reveal hidden opponent cards, private opponent state or future random outcomes.

When an effect cannot be predicted without exposing hidden information or consuming randomness:

- Display the known portion of the effect.
- Identify the uncertain portion.
- Explain that it resolves after placement.

Do not simulate random outcomes and present them as guaranteed.

Do not expose the authoritative random seed or future random sequence.

Ensure repeated preview requests cannot be used to discover hidden information.

## 9. Preview consistency

For deterministic, fully visible actions, the preview must agree with the actual accepted gameplay result.

Test preview versus execution for:

- Ordinary placement.
- Placement influence.
- Ownership changes.
- Power modifications.
- Evolution.
- Destruction.
- Ability chains.
- Conditional effects.
- Edge and corner placements.
- Full or nearly full battlefields.

Where actual resolution includes uncertain effects, verify that the preview accurately communicates that uncertainty.

## 10. Performance

The preview system must remain responsive on ordinary computers and mobile browsers.

Avoid issuing unrestricted server requests for every pointer movement.

Only recalculate when relevant state changes, such as:

- Selected card.
- Target tile.
- Match revision.
- Relevant player action.

Use memoization, bounded evaluation or equivalent techniques where appropriate.

Discard outdated asynchronous responses.

Do not allow expensive preview requests to become an abuse or denial-of-service opportunity.

## 11. Accessibility and visual clarity

Provide:

- High-contrast highlights.
- Distinct effect symbols.
- Readable numerical changes.
- Keyboard-accessible targeting.
- Touchscreen support.
- Reduced-motion compatibility.
- Clear selected and invalid states.

Ensure highlights remain recognizable without relying solely on color perception.

Maintain the established no-primary-scroll battlefield viewport.

## 12. Automated testing

Test:

- Non-mutating previews.
- Match revision validation.
- Legal destination detection.
- Invalid destination explanations.
- All supported placement patterns.
- Ability preview accuracy.
- Evolution previews.
- Multiple simultaneous effects.
- Hidden information protection.
- Randomness preservation.
- Cancellation.
- Rapid target changes.
- Stale asynchronous responses.
- Touch and keyboard interaction.
- Preview-to-execution consistency.
- Existing gameplay regression.
- Absence of unintended economy rewards.

Use compiled browser tests and deterministic engine fixtures.

## 13. Acceptance criteria

V1.6D is complete when:

1. Selecting a card identifies legal destinations.
2. Targeting a destination highlights actual affected tiles.
3. Predicted numerical changes are readable.
4. Ability effects are understandable before placement.
5. Evolution and other special legal placements are identified.
6. Previewing never changes authoritative match state.
7. Hidden and randomized outcomes remain protected.
8. Deterministic previews agree with actual gameplay resolution.
9. Desktop, touchscreen and keyboard interactions function.
10. Existing game rules and artwork remain unchanged.
11. Automated tests pass.

Deliver implementation summary, screenshots, representative preview examples, test results, known limitations and manual acceptance checklist.

Commit and push validated work.

Do not automatically deploy to homeserver.
