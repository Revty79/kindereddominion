# Kindred Dominion — V1.6E
## Opponent Card Placement, Action Presentation and Gameplay Resolution Animations

Repository: `Revty79/kindereddominion`

Implement a complete reusable gameplay presentation system that visibly communicates player and opponent actions in their authoritative resolution order.

The owner specifically requires that opponent cards no longer simply appear on the battlefield without understandable movement or effect presentation.

## 1. Prerequisites

Inspect:

- Existing persistent match architecture.
- Authoritative gameplay action resolution.
- Computer opponent action generation.
- Existing action and event histories.
- V1.6C card placement interactions.
- V1.6D tactical preview presentation.
- Existing scoring and turn transitions.

Preserve the established game engine.

Do not make visual animations responsible for gameplay outcomes.

## 2. Gameplay presentation events

Implement a reusable presentation-event model derived from authoritative accepted gameplay actions.

Capture sufficient information to communicate:

- Action identity.
- Actor.
- Played card identity when publicly visible.
- Origin.
- Destination.
- Previous visible state.
- Resulting visible state.
- Placement influence.
- Ability activation.
- Power changes.
- Ownership changes.
- Destruction.
- Evolution.
- Score changes.
- Turn transition.

Use existing authoritative event ordering wherever available.

If additional presentation metadata is required, extend the existing result model compatibly.

Do not fabricate intermediate events that contradict actual gameplay resolution.

Do not expose hidden cards or private opponent decisions.

## 3. Opponent turn opening

When the opponent's turn begins:

- Clearly indicate the active opponent.
- Display a lightweight thinking or decision state when appropriate.
- Prevent player action submissions where existing turn rules prohibit them.

Do not add unnecessary artificial delays.

The computer opponent's actual action should remain determined by the existing game engine.

## 4. Opponent card selection and movement

When an accepted opponent placement action is received:

- Visually identify that a card has been selected from the opponent's hand.
- Reveal only the card that becomes publicly visible through the accepted action.
- Animate the played card from the opponent's hand or representative origin.
- Move it smoothly toward its destination.
- Clearly identify the destination.
- Settle the card into its battlefield position.

Do not reveal the opponent's remaining hidden hand.

Do not imply knowledge of the exact hidden hand position when that information is unavailable.

Use the existing full-bleed card renderer for revealed cards.

## 5. Placement influence presentation

After the card reaches its destination, communicate its placement influence.

Animate or highlight:

- Affected tiles.
- Direction of influence.
- Placement token increases or decreases.
- Territory ownership changes.
- Other applicable placement consequences.

Reuse the effect classification and visual language developed in V1.6D.

The resolved presentation must correspond to the accepted action result, not a speculative preview.

## 6. Ability resolution

Present actual ability activations in a meaningful sequence.

For supported existing abilities, show:

- Source card.
- Affected card or tile.
- Ability name or concise description.
- Relevant numerical changes.
- Resulting state.

Support reusable animations for:

- Power increases.
- Power reductions.
- Destruction.
- Evolution.
- Replacement.
- Protection.
- Territory changes.
- Other existing effect categories.

Avoid requiring unique expensive animations for every individual card.

Provide reusable effect primitives that future cards can use through their existing gameplay definitions.

## 7. Evolution and destruction

Evolution must visibly communicate:

- The original card.
- The transformation event.
- The resulting card.
- Relevant power or ability changes.

Destruction must visibly communicate:

- Which card was affected.
- The destruction event.
- The resulting battlefield state.

Do not remove cards from the presentation before the player has a reasonable opportunity to identify what happened.

Preserve authoritative card identity and ownership rules.

## 8. Score presentation

When an accepted action changes scoring:

- Clearly identify affected scoring areas.
- Animate visible numerical changes.
- Distinguish previous values from new values.
- Settle on the authoritative resulting score.

Do not modify scoring calculations.

Do not delay authoritative result persistence merely to animate a number.

## 9. Player action presentation

Apply the same effect resolution system to the player's accepted actions.

V1.6C handles selecting and placing the player's card.

V1.6E should communicate the consequences after the accepted placement, including influence, abilities and score changes.

Avoid duplicating the initial placement animation.

Player and opponent actions should use consistent presentation rules.

## 10. Authoritative state versus visual state

Maintain a clear separation between:

- Authoritative persistent match state.
- Currently displayed animation state.
- Queued presentation events.
- Player input availability.

Authoritative action settlement must not depend on whether animations complete.

If the player disconnects, closes the browser or experiences an animation failure, the accepted match state must remain recoverable.

Animations must never trigger actual gameplay settlement a second time.

When presentation cannot complete, safely reconcile to the authoritative latest state.

## 11. Turn transitions

Return control to the player after the relevant presentation sequence finishes or is intentionally skipped.

Do not allow duplicate player actions during presentation.

Support future multiplayer requirements where authoritative turn timers may continue independently of animations.

The visual layer must never extend or alter authoritative turn deadlines.

If incoming authoritative events arrive while another animation is playing, queue or reconcile them safely.

## 12. Animation timing

Provide reusable timing settings and bounded animation durations.

Avoid excessively long forced animations.

Initial animation timings should be brief enough to maintain enjoyable match pacing while allowing players to follow tactical consequences.

Keep timings configurable.

Do not hardcode individual animation delays throughout unrelated gameplay components.

Full user-facing animation preferences will be completed during V1.6F.

## 13. Performance

Keep presentation lightweight.

Prefer:

- Transform animations.
- Opacity transitions.
- Bounded particles and effects.
- Reusable visual primitives.
- Efficient state updates.

Avoid unnecessary full-battlefield rendering during each animation frame.

Support ordinary desktop hardware, tablets and mobile devices.

Maintain the existing gameplay viewport behavior.

## 14. Future multiplayer compatibility

Design the presentation layer to consume accepted actions independently of whether they originated from:

- A local player.
- A computer opponent.
- A future remote human opponent.
- A future spectator or replay session.

Do not implement multiplayer networking during this milestone.

Do not make presentation depend directly on computer opponent implementation details.

## 15. Automated testing

Test:

- Opponent card placement.
- Player card placement.
- Event ordering.
- Placement influence resolution.
- Ability presentation.
- Power changes.
- Evolution.
- Destruction.
- Score changes.
- Turn transitions.
- Rapid consecutive actions.
- Interrupted animation.
- Browser focus loss.
- Match restoration.
- Hidden hand protection.
- Duplicate event delivery.
- Authoritative state reconciliation.
- Reduced motion.
- Desktop and touchscreen viewport behavior.
- Existing gameplay regression.
- No duplicate economy settlement.

Include compiled browser journeys demonstrating opponent moves from selection through final resolution.

## 16. Acceptance criteria

V1.6E is complete when:

1. Opponent card placements are visibly animated.
2. Players can follow where the opponent placed a card.
3. Actual affected tiles illuminate.
4. Ability consequences are presented clearly.
5. Evolution and destruction events are understandable.
6. Score changes are visually communicated.
7. Turn transitions remain correct.
8. Hidden opponent information remains protected.
9. Animation interruption cannot corrupt gameplay.
10. Existing gameplay rules remain unchanged.
11. Presentation is reusable for future multiplayer.
12. Automated tests pass.

Deliver implementation summary, screenshots or short visual recordings where available, test results, known limitations and owner acceptance checklist.

Commit and push validated work.

Do not automatically deploy to homeserver.
