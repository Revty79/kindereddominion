# V1.2 architecture and assumptions

## Preservation and separation

Baseline commit: `f953daadb9e0373f153491048b1d3d35de1aba88`. Infrastructure checkpoint: `5335182dfdf3e195b0e9b3356fd9ff0cd6e94f3c`. Original `src/data/cards.ts`, `decks.ts`, defaults and engine outcomes remain intact. Golden fixtures generated from the original engine compare every action signature in six complete Classic/Domination games. Optional observers add diagnostics without adding public events or changing transitions.

`engine/` remains independent of React, storage, network and clocks. Content, registry/validation, Studio, persistence, sandbox, simulation, replay and assets are separate modules. The one-ply AI is unchanged. Collections use content identifiers rather than a five-name engine union.

`GameHost` adds missing bundled versions without replacing an owner working draft. Fresh workspaces use 150 cards for design; ordinary gameplay defaults to V1.1. Explicit activation affects new matches. Each match snapshots its library/artwork. StrictMode rechecks share an initialization promise; concurrent installations retry against committed revisions. Real stale-tab edits fail instead of overwriting newer work.

## Content, persistence and recovery

Content schema 1 includes definitions, metadata, collections, named decks, rulesets and optional artwork references. Version kinds distinguish baseline, draft, checkpoint, testing and archived content; card statuses distinguish draft/provisional/approved/archived. Commercial approval is not inferred from test content.

Stable identifiers survive display-name changes. Archiving retains dependencies. Restoring first retains an automatic recovery draft. An accidental exact evolution cycle can remain a blocked draft for correction, but cannot be checkpointed as testing or activated. Structurally invalid imports are rejected.

IndexedDB `kindred-dominion-studio`, version 1, stores current and previous workspace snapshots in one transaction. Writes verify the expected revision. The UI commits only after storage succeeds. LocalStorage retains the original match/custom-deck store, with version-specific drafts and V1.1 save compatibility.

Transfers use `format: kindred-dominion-studio`, schema 1, engine version `1.1.0`, entity type and structured data. Backups include all supported entities and experimental artwork. Imports reject unsafe keys, incompatible formats, invalid references and malformed candidates before committing. Conflict replacement is explicit. JSON imports are bounded to 50 MB, with bounded lists/nesting. Local persistence is not an encrypted secret store or cross-device account.

## Registry and forms

The registry exposes nine triggers, 15 effect variants, supported targets, three condition categories and two protections. Enhancement/weakening share a signed power effect. Forms, descriptions and validation use that registry; arbitrary scripts are not supported.

Targets include self, triggering creature, all eligible positions and relative patterns with relationship/classification/source-exclusion filters. Predecessor alternatives are OR within lists and AND across categories. Exact cycles block activation. Classification routes including evolved forms warn because repeatable owner strategies can be legitimate. Graph layout is memoized/bounded.

Studio safety bounds: 1–20 rows, 2–30 columns, at most 300 cells; ranks I–III; token/fortification caps up to 20; deck size up to 50 and hand up to 25, with sufficient cards for a full mulligan. Approved defaults remain 3×5, 15 cards and five opening cards. Universal generation, resurrection and resolution safeguards stay enforced in Free Play.

Descriptions, flavor and rarity are metadata; they do not execute mechanics or change stats. Images are independent of text, borders and influence diagrams.

## Sandbox and diagnostics

Construction uses ordinary state with unique instance IDs and explicit zones/lineages. It can bypass placement prerequisites while arranging a scenario. Validation checks coordinates, ownership, zones, live power, stack eligibility, RNG, identities, counters and ceilings. Execution calls the same `act`, legality checks and resolver as normal play.

Construction begins at a turn's action step, avoiding premature start effects; subsequent transfers resolve them normally. Saved scenarios freeze definitions. Applying different content/rules is explicit and revalidates; incompatible dimensions require a new board.

Observers capture actual snapshots at requirements, trigger/condition/limit decisions, influence, targeting, effect boundaries, resurrection and commit. The inspector compares recorded snapshots and reports territory, instance, zone, modifier/protection changes. Intermediate views are read-only and cannot interrupt an atomic action. No hypothetical states are inferred from text logs.

## Replay

Records retain seed/config/scoring, starter, decks, exact initial state, immutable definitions, content/engine version, actions including mulligans, turn indices, signatures, final result and historical artwork references/previews. Reconstruction verifies every signature. Definition edits cannot alter history. Signatures are regression checksums, not authentication claims.

New normal records include initialization/mulligans. Legacy recovery and sandbox records start from their explicit supplied snapshot. Both hands are visible in developer replay; ordinary handoffs remain private. Incompatible engine/schema or divergence is reported explicitly.

## Simulation and statistics

The browser runner is a Web Worker. It validates inputs, alternates starters, mirrors seats, supports single/round-robin pairings including self matches, and yields between games for cancellation. Progress/partial saves remain responsive. Cancellation happens between atomic matches and retains prior outcomes.

The action ceiling is separate from normal completion. Legitimate wins/draws, timeouts, engine faults and invariant failures remain distinct; loop safeguards are separately tracked. Failed/timed-out games are excluded from competitive denominators. Every failure retains its reproducible seed/request; at most ten retain verbose replay history, and three completed examples are sampled. Thousands of full histories are not accumulated.

Deck counts represent seat appearances; a self match contributes two. Card inclusion counts deck slots; play counts placements, with resurrection separate. Evolved counts predecessors entering stacks. Territory counts actual logged influence changes. Win/loss associations describe inclusion, not causation. Match length counts actions, including mulligans. Elapsed time/report IDs are excluded from deterministic digests.

## Founding content and assets

The provisional library follows the recommended distribution exactly in each Kindred: 12/7/4 standard ranks, three replacements and four evolutions. Original definitions are reused verbatim. Pure starters use 15 unique cards. Venom is immediate weakening; no ongoing poison status is invented.

Official files are immutable checksummed assets in `public/assets/`, with masters in `artwork-source/`. Metadata references IDs/revisions, without embedding official images. Experiments retain source and optimized WebP locally. SVG uses a strict geometric allowlist. Source/optimized paths/hashes are versioned; Git attributes preserve exact bytes across operating systems.

Images load lazily with a packaged fallback. Masters are excluded from client memory and `dist/`. Relative build paths support a future mobile wrapper without platform files. See [artwork guide](V1.2-ARTWORK.md) for commands and limits.

## Scope and review limits

No hosting, multiplayer, accounts, payments, acquisition, ranking, progression, tournaments, mobile packaging, final art or advanced AI was added. Timed statuses, scripts, interactive ability-target prompts and additional scoring methods remain unsupported. The original player interface was not redesigned.

Owner playtesting and commercial/artistic approval remain human review. The image publishing pipeline is implemented; audio/animation directories provide standard destinations, while audio authoring/playback and animation editing are not advertised as functioning controls. Large local artwork and transfer sizes are bounded and should be exported/committed regularly.
